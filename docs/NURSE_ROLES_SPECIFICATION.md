# Nurse Roles & Workflows in Pediatric Healthcare

## Overview

Nurses are the backbone of hospital operations, providing 24/7 patient care, medication administration, vital sign monitoring, and serving as the primary communication bridge between patients, families, and physicians. In pediatric settings like Rady Children's Hospital, nurses have specialized skills for working with children and their families.

---

## 🏥 Nursing Roles in Hospitals

### 1. Registered Nurse (RN)
**Primary Responsibilities:**
- Direct patient care and assessment
- Medication administration (IV, oral, topical, injections)
- Vital signs monitoring (heart rate, blood pressure, temperature, O2 saturation)
- Patient education and family communication
- Care plan implementation and documentation
- Wound care and dressing changes
- IV line management and blood draws
- Coordination with physicians, specialists, and support staff

**Pediatric-Specific:**
- Age-appropriate communication with children
- Family-centered care approach
- Growth and development monitoring
- Pain assessment using pediatric scales (FLACC, Wong-Baker)
- Parental education and support

### 2. Licensed Practical Nurse (LPN) / Licensed Vocational Nurse (LVN)
**Responsibilities:**
- Basic patient care under RN supervision
- Vital signs and routine monitoring
- Medication administration (limited scope)
- Wound care and bandage changes
- Patient hygiene assistance
- Documentation and charting

### 3. Charge Nurse
**Responsibilities:**
- Shift leadership and staff coordination
- Patient assignments and workload balancing
- Resource management (beds, equipment, supplies)
- Escalation point for clinical issues
- Communication with nursing supervisor
- Conflict resolution
- Quality assurance oversight

### 4. Nurse Practitioner (NP) / Advanced Practice Registered Nurse (APRN)
**Responsibilities:**
- Independent patient assessment and diagnosis
- Prescribing medications
- Ordering and interpreting diagnostic tests
- Developing treatment plans
- Performing procedures (suturing, intubation, central lines)
- Consulting with physicians
- Patient and family education

### 5. Clinical Nurse Specialist (CNS)
**Responsibilities:**
- Expert clinical consultation
- Staff education and training
- Quality improvement initiatives
- Evidence-based practice implementation
- Policy and protocol development
- Research and outcomes tracking

### 6. Pediatric Nurse Specialist
**Specialized Areas:**
- **PICU Nurse** - Pediatric Intensive Care Unit
- **NICU Nurse** - Neonatal Intensive Care Unit
- **Pediatric Oncology Nurse** - Cancer care
- **Pediatric Emergency Nurse** - ER/Trauma
- **Pediatric Surgical Nurse** - Pre/post-operative care
- **School Nurse** - Outpatient/preventive care

## 📋 Daily Nursing Workflows

### Shift Start (Day Shift: 7 AM - 7 PM / Night Shift: 7 PM - 7 AM)

```
1. Shift Handoff Report (30 min)
   - Receive patient status from outgoing nurse
   - Review overnight events, medication changes
   - Identify high-priority patients
   - Note pending orders and procedures

2. Initial Patient Rounds (1-2 hours)
   - Visual assessment of each patient
   - Check IV lines, drains, monitors
   - Verify patient identity and allergies
   - Review medication administration record (MAR)
   - Check for new orders

3. Medication Pass (Throughout shift)
   - 8 AM medications
   - 12 PM medications
   - 4 PM medications
   - PRN (as-needed) medications
   - Document administration in EHR

4. Vital Signs Monitoring
   - Q4H (every 4 hours) for stable patients
   - Q1H (hourly) for critical patients
   - Continuous monitoring for ICU patients

5. Documentation (Continuous)
   - Nursing assessments
   - Interventions performed
   - Patient responses
   - Family interactions
   - Incident reports (if applicable)

6. Shift End Handoff
   - Summarize patient status
   - Highlight concerns and pending items
   - Transfer responsibility to incoming nurse
```

### Inpatient Medication Administration

#### Overview
Inpatient medication administration requires rigorous safety protocols due to higher acuity patients, complex medication regimens, and 24/7 care requirements.

#### Medication Categories by Risk Level

| Risk Level | Medication Types | Verification Required |
|------------|------------------|----------------------|
| **High-Alert** | Insulin, Opioids, Anticoagulants, Chemotherapy | 2-nurse independent verification |
| **Moderate** | Antibiotics IV, Cardiac meds, Sedatives | Standard 5-Rights check |
| **Standard** | Oral medications, Vitamins, PRN meds | Standard 5-Rights check |

#### Inpatient Medication Workflow

```
1. ORDER RECEIVED
   └── Physician enters order in EHR
   └── Pharmacy reviews and verifies
   └── Medication dispensed to unit

2. PRE-ADMINISTRATION
   └── Nurse receives alert for scheduled med
   └── Reviews patient allergies
   └── Checks for drug interactions
   └── Verifies lab values (K+, Cr, INR if applicable)
   └── Confirms patient NPO status if relevant

3. BEDSIDE VERIFICATION (5 Rights + 3)
   ├── Right Patient (2 identifiers: name + DOB/MRN)
   ├── Right Medication (compare to MAR)
   ├── Right Dose (calculate weight-based if pediatric)
   ├── Right Route (PO, IV, IM, SQ, topical, etc.)
   ├── Right Time (within 30-60 min window)
   ├── Right Documentation
   ├── Right Reason (indication matches)
   └── Right Response (expected outcome)

4. ADMINISTRATION
   └── Scan patient wristband (barcode)
   └── Scan medication barcode
   └── System confirms match
   └── Administer medication
   └── Observe patient response

5. POST-ADMINISTRATION
   └── Document in MAR immediately
   └── Note any adverse reactions
   └── Set reminder for PRN re-assessment
   └── Monitor for expected effect
```

#### Pediatric Dosing Considerations

```
Weight-Based Calculations:
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Formula: Dose = (mg/kg) × Patient Weight (kg)

Example: Amoxicillin 25mg/kg for 15kg child
         = 25 × 15 = 375mg per dose

Safety Checks:
- Verify weight is current (<30 days old)
- Confirm dose does not exceed adult maximum
- Double-check decimal points (10x errors are common)
- Use standardized concentrations when possible
```

#### High-Alert Medication Protocols

**Insulin Administration:**
```
Pre-Administration:
├── Verify blood glucose level (within 1 hour)
├── Check sliding scale or scheduled dose
├── Independent double-check by 2nd RN:
│   ├── Insulin type (Regular, NPH, Lantus, etc.)
│   ├── Dose in units
│   ├── Syringe/pen reading
│   └── Patient identification
└── Verify patient will eat (for meal-time insulin)

Administration:
├── Subcutaneous injection
├── Rotate injection sites
└── Document site used

Post-Administration:
├── Monitor for hypoglycemia signs
├── Recheck glucose per protocol (usually 2-4 hours)
└── Document response
```

**Opioid Administration:**
```
Pre-Administration:
├── Pain assessment (0-10 scale or FLACC for peds)
├── Sedation score (Pasero or Richmond scale)
├── Respiratory rate (must be >12 for adults, age-based for peds)
├── Check last dose timing
├── Verify no CYP450 interactions
└── 2-RN verification for IV opioids

Administration:
├── IV: Slow push over 2-5 minutes
├── PO: With food if tolerated
└── PCA: Verify pump settings

Post-Administration:
├── Reassess pain at 15-30 min (IV) or 60 min (PO)
├── Monitor sedation and respiratory status
├── Document effectiveness
└── Naloxone at bedside if high-risk
```

### Outpatient Medication Management

#### Overview
Outpatient nursing focuses on patient education, medication reconciliation, and ensuring safe transition from hospital to home.

#### Discharge Medication Workflow

```
1. MEDICATION RECONCILIATION
   └── Compare home medications to hospital medications
   └── Identify new, changed, or discontinued meds
   └── Resolve discrepancies with physician
   └── Update medication list in EHR

2. PATIENT/FAMILY EDUCATION
   └── Review each medication:
       ├── Name (brand and generic)
       ├── Purpose ("This is for your infection")
       ├── Dose and frequency
       ├── Special instructions (with food, avoid sunlight)
       ├── Common side effects
       └── When to call doctor
   └── Demonstrate administration (inhalers, injections)
   └── Provide written instructions
   └── Use teach-back method

3. PRESCRIPTION COORDINATION
   └── Confirm prescriptions sent to pharmacy
   └── Verify insurance coverage
   └── Arrange prior authorizations if needed
   └── Provide samples or vouchers if cost barrier

4. FOLLOW-UP PLANNING
   └── Schedule follow-up appointments
   └── Arrange lab monitoring (INR, drug levels)
   └── Provide 24/7 contact information
   └── Set up home health if needed
```

#### Outpatient Infusion Center Nursing

```
Common Outpatient Infusions:
├── Chemotherapy
├── Biologic therapies (Remicade, Humira)
├── IV antibiotics (OPAT - Outpatient Parenteral Antimicrobial Therapy)
├── Blood transfusions
├── Iron infusions
├── IVIG (Immunoglobulin)
└── Hydration therapy

Pre-Infusion Assessment:
├── Vital signs baseline
├── Weight (for dosing)
├── Lab review (CBC, metabolic panel)
├── Allergy verification
├── IV access assessment
└── Pre-medications ordered (antihistamines, steroids)

During Infusion:
├── Monitor for reactions q15min × 1hr, then q30min

Post-Infusion:
├── Final vital signs

```

### 🩸 Blood Transfusion Protocols

#### Pre-Transfusion Requirements

```
PHYSICIAN ORDER MUST INCLUDE:
├── Blood product type (PRBC, Platelets, FFP, Cryo)
├── Number of units
├── Transfusion rate
├── Pre-medications (if ordered)
└── Special requirements (irradiated, CMV-negative, leukoreduced)

NURSING PRE-TRANSFUSION CHECKLIST:
□ Consent signed and in chart
□ Type and Screen or Type and Crossmatch completed
□ Blood product arrived from blood bank
□ Patient has patent IV access (18-20 gauge preferred)
□ Baseline vital signs obtained
□ Pre-medications administered (30 min prior if ordered)
□ Blood warmer set up (if indicated)
□ Emergency equipment available (O2, suction, crash cart)
```

#### Blood Product Verification (CRITICAL STEP)

```
TWO-NURSE VERIFICATION AT BEDSIDE:
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Both nurses independently verify:

1. PATIENT IDENTIFICATION
   ├── Ask patient to state name and DOB
   ├── Compare to wristband
   └── Compare to blood bank tag

2. BLOOD PRODUCT LABEL
   ├── Patient name matches
   ├── MRN matches
   ├── Blood type compatible (ABO/Rh)
   ├── Unit number matches blood bank slip
   ├── Expiration date valid
   └── Product type matches order

3. CROSSMATCH COMPATIBILITY
   └── Blood bank compatibility label attached

⚠️ ANY DISCREPANCY = DO NOT TRANSFUSE
   Return to blood bank immediately
```

#### Transfusion Administration

```
INITIATION:
├── Start infusion slowly: 25-50 mL in first 15 minutes
├── Stay with patient for first 15 minutes
├── Vital signs: Baseline, 15 min, 30 min, then hourly
└── Assess for transfusion reactions

TRANSFUSION RATES:
| Product | Initial Rate | Max Rate | Max Time |
|---------|-------------|----------|----------|
| PRBC | 25-50 mL/hr × 15min | 200-250 mL/hr | 4 hours |
| Platelets | 60-100 mL over 30min | - | 4 hours |
| FFP | 200-250 mL/hr | 500 mL/hr | 4 hours |
| Cryo | Push or 10 mL/min | - | - |

PEDIATRIC RATES:
├── PRBC: 10-15 mL/kg over 2-4 hours
├── Platelets: 10 mL/kg over 30-60 min
└── FFP: 10-15 mL/kg over 1-2 hours
```

#### Transfusion Reaction Management

```
SIGNS OF TRANSFUSION REACTION:
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Acute (within minutes to 24 hours):
├── Fever (≥1°C rise), chills, rigors
├── Urticaria, flushing, itching
├── Dyspnea, wheezing, hypoxia
├── Hypotension or hypertension
├── Chest pain, back pain, flank pain
├── Nausea, vomiting
├── Dark urine (hemolysis)
└── Anxiety, sense of impending doom

IMMEDIATE ACTIONS:
1. STOP TRANSFUSION IMMEDIATELY
2. Keep IV line open with normal saline
3. Call for help / Notify physician STAT
4. Check vital signs
5. Verify patient ID and blood product (clerical error check)
6. Assess airway, breathing, circulation
7. Administer emergency medications as ordered:
   ├── Epinephrine (anaphylaxis)
   ├── Diphenhydramine (allergic)
   ├── Hydrocortisone (allergic/TRALI)
   └── Furosemide (TACO - fluid overload)
8. Send blood bag and tubing to blood bank
9. Collect post-reaction blood and urine samples
10. Complete transfusion reaction report
```

#### Transfusion Documentation

```
REQUIRED DOCUMENTATION:
├── Pre-transfusion vital signs and assessment
├── Two-nurse verification signatures
├── Time transfusion started
├── Vital signs at 15 min, 30 min, hourly, completion
├── Total volume transfused
├── Time transfusion completed
├── Post-transfusion assessment
├── Any reactions and interventions
└── Patient tolerance and response
```

### 💉 IV Therapy & Titration Protocols

#### IV Access Types

| Access Type | Gauge | Uses | Max Duration |
|-------------|-------|------|--------------|
| Peripheral IV | 22-24G (peds), 18-20G (adults) | Fluids, meds, blood | 72-96 hours |
| Midline | 18-22G | Extended IV therapy | 1-4 weeks |
| PICC | 3-5 Fr | Long-term IV, TPN, chemo | Months |
| Central Line | 7-9 Fr | ICU, vasopressors, TPN | Weeks-months |
| Port-a-cath | Implanted | Chemo, long-term access | Years |

#### IV Fluid Management

```
COMMON IV FLUIDS:
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
| Fluid | Tonicity | Indications |
|-------|----------|-------------|
| NS (0.9% NaCl) | Isotonic | Resuscitation, blood compatible |
| LR (Lactated Ringers) | Isotonic | Surgical, trauma, burns |
| D5W | Hypotonic | Medication diluent, free water |
| D5NS | Hypertonic | Maintenance with glucose |
| D5 1/2NS | Hypotonic | Pediatric maintenance |
| 3% Saline | Hypertonic | Severe hyponatremia (ICU only) |

PEDIATRIC MAINTENANCE FLUIDS (4-2-1 Rule):
├── First 10 kg: 4 mL/kg/hr
├── Next 10 kg: 2 mL/kg/hr
└── Each kg >20: 1 mL/kg/hr

Example: 25 kg child
= (4×10) + (2×10) + (1×5)
= 40 + 20 + 5 = 65 mL/hr maintenance
```

#### IV Medication Titration

**Vasoactive Drip Titration (ICU):**
```
COMMON TITRATED MEDICATIONS:
├── Vasopressors: Norepinephrine, Vasopressin, Epinephrine
├── Inotropes: Dobutamine, Milrinone, Dopamine
├── Vasodilators: Nitroprusside, Nitroglycerin
├── Sedatives: Propofol, Dexmedetomidine, Midazolam
├── Analgesics: Fentanyl, Morphine, Hydromorphone
└── Antiarrhythmics: Amiodarone, Lidocaine

TITRATION PRINCIPLES:
1. Start at lowest effective dose
2. Titrate to clinical goal (MAP, HR, pain score)
3. Make incremental changes (10-20% adjustments)
4. Allow time between adjustments (5-15 min for pressors)
5. Document every rate change with indication
6. Watch for adverse effects at higher doses
```

**Example: Norepinephrine Titration**
```
Goal: MAP ≥65 mmHg

Starting: 0.05 mcg/kg/min
├── If MAP <65: Increase by 0.02-0.05 mcg/kg/min q5-10min
├── If MAP >80: Consider decreasing by 0.02 mcg/kg/min
├── Max dose: 0.5-2 mcg/kg/min (varies by protocol)
└── Wean when stable × 2-4 hours, decrease by 0.02-0.05 q15-30min

MONITORING:
├── Continuous arterial BP (A-line preferred)
├── Heart rate and rhythm
├── Urine output (goal >0.5 mL/kg/hr)
├── Lactate levels
├── Peripheral perfusion (capillary refill, skin color)
└── Central venous access site assessment
```

**Insulin Drip Titration (DKA/HHS):**
```
STANDARD PROTOCOL:
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Starting: 0.1 units/kg/hr IV (after bolus if ordered)

Titration based on glucose drop rate:
├── Glucose dropping 50-75 mg/dL/hr: Continue same rate
├── Glucose dropping <50 mg/dL/hr: Increase by 0.02 units/kg/hr
├── Glucose dropping >75 mg/dL/hr: Decrease by 50%
└── Glucose <250: Start D5 in fluids, decrease insulin to 0.05 units/kg/hr

MONITORING:
├── Blood glucose q1h
├── Basic metabolic panel q2-4h
├── Anion gap calculation
├── Potassium replacement (insulin drives K+ into cells)
└── Neuro checks (cerebral edema risk in peds)
```

**Heparin Drip Titration:**
```
WEIGHT-BASED PROTOCOL:
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Bolus: 80 units/kg (max 10,000 units)
Initial rate: 18 units/kg/hr

Titration based on aPTT (goal 60-100 sec):
| aPTT | Bolus | Rate Change |
|------|-------|-------------|
| <50 | 40 units/kg | ↑ 4 units/kg/hr |
| 50-59 | 0 | ↑ 2 units/kg/hr |
| 60-100 | 0 | No change |
| 101-120 | 0 | ↓ 2 units/kg/hr |
| 121-150 | 0, hold 30 min | ↓ 3 units/kg/hr |
| >150 | 0, hold 60 min | ↓ 4 units/kg/hr |

MONITORING:
├── aPTT q6h until therapeutic, then daily
├── CBC daily (watch for HIT - Heparin Induced Thrombocytopenia)
├── Signs of bleeding
└── Platelet count (stop if drops >50%)
```

#### IV Site Assessment

```
ASSESS EVERY SHIFT (or per protocol):
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Use the "DIPS" Mnemonic:

D - Dressing (clean, dry, intact, dated)
I - Infiltration/Infection signs:
    ├── Redness, swelling, warmth
    ├── Pain or tenderness
    ├── Drainage or purulence
    └── Streak marks (phlebitis)
P - Patency (flushes easily, no resistance)
S - Securement (tubing secured, no tension)

PHLEBITIS SCALE:
| Grade | Signs | Action |
|-------|-------|--------|
| 0 | No symptoms | Continue monitoring |
| 1 | Pain at site | Monitor closely |
| 2 | Pain + redness | Consider removal |
| 3 | Pain + redness + streak | Remove, notify MD |
| 4 | Pain + redness + streak + palpable cord | Remove, notify MD, cultures |

INFILTRATION SCALE:
| Grade | Signs | Action |
|-------|-------|--------|
| 1 | Skin blanched, cool, edema <1 inch | Discontinue, elevate |
| 2 | Skin blanched, cool, edema 1-6 inches | Discontinue, warm compress |
| 3 | Edema >6 inches, mild pain | Remove, notify MD |
| 4 | Edema, tissue damage, blistering | Remove, antidote if applicable, notify MD |
```

### ❤️ EKG/ECG Monitoring & Interpretation

#### Continuous Cardiac Monitoring Indications

```
WHEN TO MONITOR:
├── Chest pain / Acute coronary syndrome
├── Arrhythmia history or new arrhythmia
├── Post-cardiac procedure (cath, ablation, pacemaker)
├── Electrolyte abnormalities (K+, Mg2+, Ca2+)
├── Drug toxicity (digoxin, antiarrhythmics)
├── Altered mental status with unknown cause
├── Post-cardiac arrest
├── High-risk medications (QT-prolonging drugs)
├── Heart failure exacerbation
└── Syncope workup
```

#### Basic EKG Interpretation for Nurses

```
SYSTEMATIC APPROACH (Rate-Rhythm-Axis-Intervals-ST/T):
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

1. RATE
   ├── Count R waves in 6-second strip × 10
   ├── Or: 300 ÷ (# large boxes between R waves)
   ├── Normal: 60-100 bpm
   ├── Bradycardia: <60 bpm
   └── Tachycardia: >100 bpm

2. RHYTHM
   ├── Regular or irregular?
   ├── P waves present before each QRS?
   ├── P waves uniform in shape?
   └── PR interval consistent?

3. P WAVE
   ├── Present/absent
   ├── Shape (peaked = right atrial hypertrophy)
   └── Association with QRS

4. PR INTERVAL (Normal: 0.12-0.20 sec / 3-5 small boxes)
   ├── Short (<0.12): WPW, junctional rhythm
   ├── Prolonged (>0.20): 1st degree AV block
   └── Variable: 2nd degree AV block

5. QRS COMPLEX (Normal: <0.12 sec / <3 small boxes)
   ├── Narrow: Supraventricular origin
   ├── Wide (>0.12): Bundle branch block, ventricular origin
   └── Absent P waves + wide QRS: V-tach until proven otherwise

6. ST SEGMENT
   ├── Elevation: STEMI (notify MD immediately)
   ├── Depression: Ischemia
   └── Should be at baseline (isoelectric)

7. T WAVE
   ├── Peaked: Hyperkalemia
   ├── Flattened: Hypokalemia
   └── Inverted: Ischemia, strain
```

#### Common Arrhythmias Recognition

```
CRITICAL ARRHYTHMIAS (NOTIFY MD IMMEDIATELY):
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

V-Fib (Ventricular Fibrillation)
├── Appearance: Chaotic, no organized rhythm
├── Action: Call code, start CPR, defibrillate
└── This is a cardiac arrest rhythm

V-Tach (Ventricular Tachycardia)
├── Appearance: Wide QRS, regular, fast (>150)
├── With pulse: Amiodarone, cardioversion
├── Without pulse: Defibrillate
└── Check patient - are they conscious?

Asystole
├── Appearance: Flat line (confirm in 2 leads)
├── Action: Call code, start CPR, epinephrine
└── Check leads are connected!

PEA (Pulseless Electrical Activity)
├── Appearance: Organized rhythm but NO pulse
├── Action: CPR, epinephrine, treat reversible causes (H's and T's)
└── Always check pulse with any rhythm change

Torsades de Pointes
├── Appearance: Twisting QRS around baseline
├── Action: Magnesium IV, defibrillate if pulseless
└── Stop QT-prolonging medications
```

```
URGENT ARRHYTHMIAS (NOTIFY MD):
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

A-Fib with RVR (Atrial Fibrillation with Rapid Ventricular Response)
├── Appearance: Irregularly irregular, no P waves, rate >110
├── Symptoms: Palpitations, dyspnea, hypotension
└── Treatment: Rate control (diltiazem, metoprolol), anticoagulation

SVT (Supraventricular Tachycardia)
├── Appearance: Narrow QRS, very regular, rate 150-250
├── Action: Vagal maneuvers, adenosine
└── Unstable: Synchronized cardioversion

Complete Heart Block (3rd Degree AV Block)
├── Appearance: P waves and QRS not related, slow rate
├── Atria and ventricles beating independently
└── May need transcutaneous pacing
```

```
COMMON ARRHYTHMIAS (MONITOR/DOCUMENT):
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Sinus Tachycardia
├── Normal P waves, regular rhythm, rate >100
└── Treat underlying cause (pain, fever, hypovolemia)

Sinus Bradycardia
├── Normal P waves, regular rhythm, rate <60
└── Often normal in athletes; treat if symptomatic

A-Fib (controlled)
├── Irregularly irregular, no P waves, rate 60-100
└── Continue rate control and anticoagulation

PVCs (Premature Ventricular Contractions)
├── Wide, bizarre QRS, compensatory pause
├── Occasional: Usually benign
└── Frequent (>6/min), runs, or R-on-T: Notify MD

PACs (Premature Atrial Contractions)
├── Early beat with different P wave shape
└── Usually benign, may indicate irritability
```
